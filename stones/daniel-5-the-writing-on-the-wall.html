Daniel 5: The Writing on the Wall

Babylon's last night, a mysterious hand, and the ruler that historians once said never existed.

Read alongside: Daniel 5

Chapter at a glance
Setting
Babylon, 539 BC, the night the city fell
Main people
Belshazzar, the queen, Daniel
Key words
MENE, MENE, TEKEL, PARSIN
Related artifact
Nabonidus Cylinder from Ur, British Museum

Daniel 5 tells one of the most dramatic scenes in the Bible. It is also a chapter that archaeology has shed new light on.

The feast

Belshazzar, called king of Babylon, threw a huge banquet for a thousand of his nobles. While drinking, he ordered servants to bring the gold and silver cups that Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple in Jerusalem decades earlier. Belshazzar, his nobles, his wives and his concubines drank wine from these holy vessels and praised gods of gold, silver, bronze, iron, wood and stone.

The hand

Suddenly the fingers of a human hand appeared and wrote on the plaster wall near the lampstand. The king turned pale and his knees knocked together. He called his wise men and promised that whoever could read the writing would be clothed in purple, wear a gold chain, and become the third ruler in the kingdom. None of them could.

The queen, probably the queen mother, came in and reminded him of Daniel, a Jewish exile whom Nebuchadnezzar had trusted because the spirit of the holy gods was in him. Daniel was brought in. He refused the rewards but agreed to read the writing.

Daniel's message

Before interpreting, Daniel reminded Belshazzar of Nebuchadnezzar's story from Daniel 4. God had humbled that great king until he acknowledged that the Most High rules over human kingdoms. Belshazzar knew all this, Daniel said, yet he had not humbled his heart. He had lifted himself up against the Lord of heaven by drinking from the temple's vessels.

Then Daniel read the words: MENE, MENE, TEKEL, PARSIN. They are Aramaic terms for weights or measures, which also sound like verbs. Daniel explained: MENE, God has numbered the days of your kingdom and brought it to an end. TEKEL, you have been weighed on the scales and found lacking. PERES, your kingdom is divided and given to the Medes and Persians. (Peres also sounds like the word for Persia.)

That same night Belshazzar was killed, and Darius the Mede received the kingdom (Daniel 5:30–31).

The history behind it

Ancient Babylonian king lists name Nabonidus as the last king of Babylon, and for centuries Belshazzar was not known from any source outside the Bible. Then, in the mid-1800s, clay cylinders were found at Ur, a city in southern Iraq. On them, Nabonidus prays for himself and for Belshazzar, his firstborn son. Other Babylonian texts show that Nabonidus spent about ten years away at the desert oasis of Tayma in Arabia, leaving Belshazzar in charge of Babylon.

This also explains a detail in Daniel 5. Belshazzar could offer only the third place in the kingdom, because Nabonidus was first and Belshazzar himself was second. Greek historians Herodotus and Xenophon also say Babylon fell during a festival, when the city was celebrating.
