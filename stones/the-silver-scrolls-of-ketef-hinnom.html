The Silver Scrolls of Ketef Hinnom

This reading opens on Monday, November 16, 2026. Come back that day to read it and take the quiz.

Go to today's reading

Two tiny rolled-up silver amulets hold the oldest known words of Scripture, the priestly blessing from Numbers 6.

Read alongside: Numbers 6:22–27

Object file
What
Two small silver amulets inscribed in ancient Hebrew
Dated
About 600 BC
Found
A burial cave at Ketef Hinnom, Jerusalem, 1979
Now at
The Israel Museum, Jerusalem

Ketef Hinnom means "shoulder of Hinnom." It is a rocky ridge on the west side of the Hinnom Valley in Jerusalem, across from Mount Zion. In 1979, a team led by archaeologist Gabriel Barkay was excavating a group of burial caves cut into the rock there.

A lucky find

Most of the caves had been robbed long ago. In one chamber, part of the ceiling had collapsed at some point, sealing a small storage area beneath the rock. When the team cleared it, they found more than a thousand objects: pottery, arrowheads, jewelry, and other items. Among them were two tiny rolled-up pieces of silver.

The larger one, when unrolled, measures less than four inches long and about an inch wide. The smaller one is even narrower. They were made to be worn, probably on a string around the neck, as protective amulets. The silver was so fragile that it took about three years of careful work to unroll them without destroying them.

What they say

When the scrolls were finally opened, they were found to be covered in tiny letters written in the ancient Hebrew script used before the Babylonian exile. Both contain words from the priestly blessing that God gave to Aaron and his sons in Numbers 6:24–26:

"The Lord bless thee, and keep thee: The Lord make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: The Lord lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace."

The scrolls use the personal name of God, YHWH, which English Bibles usually write as "the Lord."

The oldest words of Scripture

Based on the style of the letters and the other objects found with them, the scrolls are dated to around 600 BC, near the end of the time of the kings of Judah, in the days of the prophet Jeremiah. That makes them the oldest known copies of any text from the Bible. They are about 400 years older than the oldest of the Dead Sea Scrolls.

Why it matters

The amulets show that people in Jerusalem knew and treasured this blessing during the time of the First Temple. Someone wanted these words close to them, even in death. Today the same blessing is still spoken in synagogues and churches around the world, more than 2,600 years after these scrolls were made.
