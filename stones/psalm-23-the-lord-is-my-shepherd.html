Psalm 23: The Lord Is My Shepherd

Six verses that have comforted readers for three thousand years, written by a king who began as a shepherd.

Read alongside: Psalm 23; John 10:11–14; 1 Samuel 16:11; 17:34–35

Chapter at a glance
Author
David, according to the heading "A Psalm of David"
Length
6 verses
Main picture
God as a shepherd and as a host
Book
Psalms, Book 1 (Psalms 1–41)

Psalm 23 is one of the best-known chapters of the Bible. Its heading calls it "A Psalm of David." Before he became king, David was a shepherd. When the prophet Samuel came to anoint one of Jesse's sons, David was out keeping the sheep (1 Samuel 16:11). Later David told King Saul that he had protected his father's flock from a lion and a bear (1 Samuel 17:34–35). David knew firsthand what a shepherd does.

The shepherd (verses 1–4)

The psalm opens, "The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want." The rest of the first half shows what that means. The shepherd makes his sheep lie down in green pastures and leads them beside still waters. He restores the soul and leads in paths of righteousness "for his name's sake."

Verse 4 turns to danger: "Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me." Here the psalm changes from speaking about God to speaking to him. The shepherd's rod and staff, tools for protecting and guiding the flock, bring comfort.

The host (verses 5–6)

In verse 5 the picture changes. God is now a generous host: "Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of mine enemies." In the ancient Near East, a host who welcomed a guest was responsible for that guest's safety. Anointing the head with oil was a sign of honor and welcome, and an overflowing cup showed abundance.

The psalm ends with confidence: "Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life: and I will dwell in the house of the Lord for ever."

The shepherd in the rest of the Bible

Shepherd language runs through Scripture. God is called the shepherd of Israel (Psalm 80:1), and Isaiah says he will feed his flock like a shepherd and carry the lambs in his arms (Isaiah 40:11). In the New Testament, Jesus says, "I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep" (John 10:11). Hebrews 13:20 calls Jesus "that great shepherd of the sheep."

Why it is so loved

Psalm 23 is often read at funerals, memorized by children, and prayed in hard times. Its words are simple, and they speak of God's care in every part of life, in daily needs, in danger, and in the future.
