Acts 2: The Day of Pentecost

This reading opens on Thursday, November 19, 2026. Come back that day to read it and take the quiz.

Go to today's reading

Wind, fire, many languages, and Peter's first sermon: the day the church began in Jerusalem.

Read alongside: Acts 2:1–47; Joel 2:28–32; Leviticus 23:15–21

Chapter at a glance
Setting
Jerusalem, the Feast of Pentecost, about AD 30
Main people
Peter and the apostles
Old Testament quoted
Joel 2; Psalm 16; Psalm 110
Result
About 3,000 people baptized (Acts 2:41)

Pentecost was one of the three great pilgrimage festivals of Israel. Its Hebrew name is Shavuot, the Feast of Weeks, because it came seven weeks after Passover (Leviticus 23:15–16). The Greek name Pentecost means "fiftieth," for the fiftieth day. At harvest time, Jews from across the Roman world and beyond crowded into Jerusalem for the feast.

Wind and fire

Before he ascended, Jesus had told his followers to wait in Jerusalem for the promised Holy Spirit (Acts 1:4–5). On the day of Pentecost they were all together in one place. Suddenly a sound came from heaven like a rushing mighty wind and filled the house. Tongues like fire appeared and rested on each of them. "And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues" (Acts 2:4).

Every nation under heaven

A crowd gathered at the sound. Luke lists where the visitors came from: Parthians, Medes, Elamites, people from Mesopotamia, Judea, Cappadocia, Pontus, Asia, Phrygia, Pamphylia, Egypt, Libya, Rome, Crete and Arabia (Acts 2:9–11). Each heard the apostles speaking in his own language about the wonderful works of God. Some were amazed. Others mocked and said the apostles were drunk.

Peter's sermon

Peter stood up with the other eleven apostles. It was only nine in the morning, he pointed out, too early for anyone to be drunk. Instead, this was what the prophet Joel had foretold: "I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh" (Acts 2:17, quoting Joel 2:28).

Peter then spoke about Jesus of Nazareth. He reminded the crowd of Jesus' miracles, his death, and his resurrection, and he quoted Psalm 16 and Psalm 110, both psalms of David. He said, "This Jesus hath God raised up, whereof we all are witnesses" (Acts 2:32).

The listeners were cut to the heart and asked, "What shall we do?" Peter answered, "Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost" (Acts 2:38). About three thousand people were baptized that day.

The first church

The chapter ends with a picture of the first believers. They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching, to fellowship, to the breaking of bread, and to prayer (Acts 2:42). They shared their possessions with anyone in need, met daily in the temple courts, and ate together in their homes with glad hearts. "And the Lord added to the church daily such as should be saved" (Acts 2:47).

Why it matters

Many Christians mark Pentecost as the birthday of the church. The list of nations in Acts 2 also previews the rest of the book of Acts, which follows the message as it spreads from Jerusalem across the Roman world.
